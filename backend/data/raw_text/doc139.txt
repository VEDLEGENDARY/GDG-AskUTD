Vaccination Requirement –
Bacterial Meningitis
About Bacterial Meningitis
Bacterial Meningitis is a serious, potentially deadly disease that can progress extremely fast. It is an
inflammation of the membranes that surround the brain and spinal cord. The bacterium that causes
meningitis can also infect the blood. For more information, please review the About Bacterial
Meningitis information.
Who Must Comply
Per Texas Senate Bill no. 62, beginning January 1, 2014, all entering students under the age of 22
enrolling at a Texas college or university must show proof of a bacterial meningitis vaccination or
booster (if the original vaccination is five years old) at least 10 days before the first day of class.
Failure to receive vaccination by the deadline dates below will prevent you from attending classes
for the term.
Acceptable Proof of Vaccination
Documentation must:
Be in English
Include student’s full name and other identifying information (i.e date of birth)
Show the month, date and year of vaccination.
Be within the five years prior to and no later than 10 days before the first day of the term.
Be one of the following:
Documentation of vaccines administered that include the signature or stamp of the
physician or physician’s designee, or public health personnel, is acceptable.
Office of the Registrar

Immunization records generated from electronic health record systems must include
clinic contact information and the provider’s signature/stamp.
An official immunization record generated from a state or local health authority.
An official record received from school officials, including a record from another state.
What you Need to Submit
To attend classes, you must submit:
1. A completed Meningococcal Meningitis Vaccine Requirement Form (pdf)
2. Proof of vaccination or a notarized Affidavit for Exemption from Immunizations for Reasons of
Conscience
Submission Deadline by Term
Your vaccine must be administered within five years prior to and no later than 10 days before the
first day of the term.
Spring 2027 Term – Meningitis vaccine must be taken between January 19, 2022 and January 9,
2027.
Summer 2027 Term – Meningitis vaccine must be taken between June 1, 2022 and May 22, 2027.
Fall 2027 Term – Meningitis vaccine must be taken between August 23, 2022 and August 13, 2027.

Exemptions
You may be exempt if:
You are 22 years or older by the first day of class for the semester
You are taking only online classes (submit the Online Enrollment Only Exemption Request form
(pdf).
You are are serving on active duty with the armed forces of the United States.
Your physician advises against vaccination for health reasons (submit a signed note on
letterhead or medical stationary. This statement should accompany the Vaccine Requirement
Form (pdf)
You decline for reasons of conscience or religious belief by submitting an affidavit.
Affidavits
In Texas, there are two ways to obtain an immunization exemption affidavit for reasons of conscience
(including religious beliefs):
1. Download the blank affidavit form directly from the Texas Department of State Health Services
(DSHS) website. A child’s parent, a legal guardian, or a student 18 years or older has the option
to download and print the blank immunization exemption affidavit form. The form must be
completed, notarized, and submitted to the Office of the Registrar.
https://www.dshs.texas.gov/immunizations/school/exemptions
2. Request a hard copy of the affidavit from the Texas Department of State Health Services.
Individuals may submit a request online, by mail, fax, or in person. Texas DSHS will mail the
official affidavit form to the address provided. Please see below to request a hard copy
exemption affidavit by one of these methods:
Secure online request form for exemption affidavit
Fax: (512) 776-7544
In person:

All affidavits must be notarized by a Texas notary. If requesting the original hard copy affidavit to be
mailed to you, UT Dallas can only accept the orignal hard copy. Both affidavits serve the same
purpose; the difference is how the affidavits are obtained. Once completed and notarized, the
affidavits must be submitted according to Texas immunization exemption requirements.
